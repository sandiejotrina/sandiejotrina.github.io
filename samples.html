Sample 1 · Prescribe

RAPID: what to automate

Score a candidate process from 1 to 4 on each of five questions. Total out of 20.

R · Repetitive
Does it happen often, the same way each time?
A · Accuracy-critical
Do errors carry real cost, risk, or rework?
P · Predictable
Are the inputs and decision rules stable and clear?
I · Integration-heavy
Does someone move data between systems by hand?
D · Documented
Is the process written down well enough to build from?
  • 15 to 20

    Automate

    Strong candidate. Move to tool selection.

  • 10 to 14

    Not ready

    Document and standardize the process first, then score again.

  • Under 10

    Redesign or leave to people

    Automation would add cost without adding value.

The guardrail: a high score tells you a process can be automated. It does not tell you it should be. The highest-scoring process is sometimes a broken one you would only be paving over. Diagnose first.

Sample 2 · Prescribe

C1 to C4: which tool, only after RAPID says yes

C1 · Capability
Can it handle the real volume, the edge cases, and the data you have?
C2 · Cost
Total cost over three years: licenses, build, support, and the people to run it.
C3 · Change
How much do people have to change how they work, and who supports them through it?
C4 · Compliance
Audit trail, validation, data privacy, and who is accountable for the output.

In practice this is how a buy option beat a custom build at about one third lower three-year cost and a 4-month implementation instead of 13. See the case study.

Sample 3 · Treat

The AI review gate

A pattern for using generative AI where accuracy and accountability matter: training content, client communication, regulated correspondence.

  1. Approved sources only. The AI drafts from a defined knowledge base, not the open internet.
  2. De-identified test data. Prove it works before real data touches it.
  3. Documented prompts. A prompt library with versions, so results can be repeated and audited.
  4. Human review before release. A named person approves every output. Nothing goes out on the AI's authority.
  5. Measure the work, not the tool. Turnaround time, error rate, and adoption, compared with the old process.

See it in practice.

Sample 4 · Follow Up

A certification path that proves readiness

One structure for any field or client-facing role. Certified means able to do the work, not finished with the modules.

  1. Foundation. The problem the product solves and who it serves. Knowledge check.
  2. Product. How it works and how it is delivered. Knowledge check.
  3. Risk. What can go wrong and how to respond. Scenario assessment.
  4. Compliance. The rules of the role, documented. Attestation on file.
  5. Readiness. Observed role play and a supervised live interaction. Signed off by a manager.
  6. Stay current. Recertify when the product, data, or rules change, not just on a calendar date.

The metric that matters: time to readiness and pass rates by role, not course completions.
