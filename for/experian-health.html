How I would start

The first 90 days: diagnose before prescribing

Most education teams answer every client problem with more content. Some of those problems are product or process gaps that no course will fix. The first 90 days sort one from the other.

  1. Days 1 to 30

    Examine. Listen to clients, implementation, and customer success. Inventory content by product. Pull Learning Portal and support data to see where clients actually get stuck.

  2. Days 31 to 60

    Diagnose and prescribe. Sort each sticking point: skill gap (teach it) or system gap (route it to product). Decide where AI speeds up content production and where people must stay in the loop.

  3. Days 61 to 90

    Treat and follow up. Pilot an AI-assisted content workflow on one product, with review built in. Agree on adoption metrics with customer success. Publish the multi-year roadmap.

A working sample

AI-assisted client education content, with a review gate

The pattern I would bring to scale content across products without losing accuracy or trust.

  1. Source. Product release notes, approved documentation, and top support questions.
  2. Draft with AI. First drafts of job aids, video scripts, and knowledge checks, from approved sources only.
  3. Review gate. A product subject matter expert and an instructional designer approve before anything publishes.
  4. Publish. To the Learning Portal, tagged by product, role, and stage: implementation or ongoing use.
  5. Measure. Completion, then the numbers that matter: time to go live, feature adoption, and support tickets per client.

The metric that matters: course completions show activity. Fewer how-to support tickets and faster time to value show impact. I would report both, and lead with impact.
