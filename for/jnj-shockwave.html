How I would start

The first 90 days: diagnose before prescribing

Not every leadership gap is a training gap. Some are territory, comp plan, or process problems that no workshop will fix. The first 90 days sort one from the other.

  1. Days 1 to 30

    Examine. Ride along with RSDs and AVPs. Interview top and struggling leaders. Inventory current programs and pull the data that already exists: rep ramp time, retention, coaching cadence.

  2. Days 31 to 60

    Diagnose and prescribe. Name the three leader behaviors that separate the best regions from the rest. Map each to skill, system, or incentive. Agree on success measures with sales leadership and HR.

  3. Days 61 to 90

    Treat and follow up. Pilot the first quarter of the path below with one area. Set the measurement baseline. Publish the full-year curriculum.

A working sample

A one-year leader development path, built around the field

Four quarters, one leader behavior each. Every quarter pairs a short Rise 360 module with practice in the field and a coaching check, so learning happens where the work happens.

  1. Quarter 1: Coach the call. The ride-along debrief in four steps: Observe without stepping in, Ask the rep what they saw first, Coach one thing that would change the next call, and Commit to it in writing before leaving the parking lot.
  2. Quarter 2: Lead with data. Turn CRM and territory data into one coaching question per rep each week, instead of a report review.
  3. Quarter 3: Lead with AI. Help leaders and reps use AI tools well and within policy: brief AI like a colleague, check its output before it reaches a customer, and keep people accountable for decisions.
  4. Quarter 4: Build the bench. Each RSD develops one future leader, with a development plan the AVP reviews.
  5. Measure. Leader behavior first (coaching visits completed, debrief quality on a simple rubric), then business signals sales leadership already tracks, such as rep ramp time and retention.

The metric that matters: attendance shows activity. Coaching that happens every week in the field, and reps who ramp faster and stay longer, show impact. I would report both, and lead with impact.
