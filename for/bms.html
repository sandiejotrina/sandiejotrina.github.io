How I would start

The first 90 days: diagnose before prescribing

Not every field gap is a training gap. Some are access, materials, or process problems that no course will fix. The first 90 days sort one from the other.

  1. Days 1 to 30

    Examine. Ride along with MSLs and field medical leaders. Inventory current neuro curricula and onboarding. Pull completion, certification, and field coaching data to see where new colleagues get stuck.

  2. Days 31 to 60

    Diagnose and prescribe. Map each gap to knowledge, skill, or system. Agree with Medical, Legal, and Regulatory on what AI may support in learning and where people stay in the loop.

  3. Days 61 to 90

    Treat and follow up. Pilot one onboarding module with practice built in, like the sample below. Set readiness metrics with field medical leadership. Publish a learning roadmap tied to the neuro portfolio and pipeline.

A working sample

Neuropsychiatry onboarding: disease state to scientific exchange

A five lesson onboarding module for new field medical colleagues, built in Articulate Rise 360. It moves from knowing the disease state to practicing the conversation.

  1. Why it matters. Schizophrenia affects about 27 million people worldwide, about 1 in 300, usually starting in late adolescence or early adulthood. People living with it die on average 9 years earlier than the general population, largely from physical illness (WHO, 2023 figures).
  2. Three symptom domains. Positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking), negative symptoms (reduced motivation, emotional expression, and social drive), and cognitive symptoms (attention, working memory, executive function). Most current antipsychotics act mainly at dopamine D2 receptors and do the most for positive symptoms, so negative and cognitive symptoms remain a major unmet need.
  3. Scientific exchange skills. A four step model for unsolicited questions: Ask to understand the real clinical question, Answer with balanced, published evidence, Balance benefits with limitations and safety, and Close by documenting and following up per company policy.
  4. Practice with an AI coach. The learner rehearses with an AI that plays a community psychiatrist asking about negative symptoms. The AI draws only on approved sources, uses no patient data, and every transcript is scored on a rubric a field medical manager reviews.
  5. Show readiness. A scenario based knowledge check, a scored practice conversation, and a manager observation before the learner works independently.

The metric that matters: completion shows activity. Time to independent field readiness and the quality of scientific exchange in coaching observations show impact. I would report both, and lead with impact.
