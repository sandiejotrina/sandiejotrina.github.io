How I start

The first 90 days: diagnose before prescribing

In medicine you never prescribe before you diagnose. Not every capability gap is a training gap. Some are role clarity, process, or incentive problems that no program will fix. The first 90 days sort one from the other.

  1. Days 1 to 30

    Examine. Go through onboarding as a new hire and take notes. Meet Talent leadership, every HR Business Partner, People Analytics, and I&D. Inventory every program, vendor, and platform, with what each costs. Pull the data that already exists: time to productivity, first-year and regretted attrition, engagement, internal promotion rates.

  2. Days 31 to 60

    Diagnose and prescribe. Name the few capabilities the strategy requires over the next three years. Sort each gap into skill, system, or incentive. Make a build, buy, curate, or retire call on every current program. Agree on the scorecard with Talent leadership and People Analytics.

  3. Days 61 to 90

    Treat and follow up. Ship one visible improvement, usually in onboarding, where the data comes back fastest. Set baselines for every major program. Publish a draft multi-year learning strategy for review.

A working model

An enterprise learning portfolio on one page

Four audiences, each with one priority capability, a modality mix chosen for how that audience actually learns at work, and two measures: a leading behavior signal and a business result the company already tracks.

Onboarding
Productive fast, and connected to the mission
Modality: short digital preboarding, a cohort week that puts new hires in front of the product and the people it serves, a manager-owned 30/60/90 plan, and nudges at the moments that matter.
Leading: 30-day manager check-in completed; new hire confidence at 30 and 90 days.
Business: time to productivity by role family; first-year retention.
Individual contributors
Use AI well and keep the judgment
Modality: on-demand paths personalized by role, practice on real work, and peer examples from inside the company.
Leading: applied use in work samples, reviewed by managers.
Business: cycle time on targeted workflows; internal mobility.
Managers
Coach, give feedback, and develop people every week
Modality: a cohort with peer practice, then twelve weeks of nudge-based reinforcement, with the manager's own leader checking in.
Leading: coaching and one-on-one frequency from the engagement pulse.
Business: team engagement and regretted attrition, compared with a baseline.
Directors
Make cross-functional trade-offs and build the bench
Modality: a small cohort working a real business problem sponsored by a senior leader, with time in front of the executive team.
Leading: quality of the sponsored project, judged by the sponsor.
Business: ready-now successors for critical roles; internal fill rate for director roles.

The build, buy, or curate filter

  1. Build when the content belongs to the company alone: its mission, culture, products, and what it expects of its people leaders.
  2. Buy when the skill is universal and a vendor already does it well at scale.
  3. Curate when good material exists. The job is to put it in front of the right person at the moment of need.
  4. Retire anything that cannot name the capability it builds or the measure it moves.
  5. Score the finalists on capability, three-year cost, change burden, and compliance before signing anything. The C1 to C4 filter

The metric that matters: completions show activity. Time to productivity, regretted attrition, and a ready bench show capability. Agree on the scorecard with People Analytics before launch, report both, and use the data to make honest calls about what to stop.
