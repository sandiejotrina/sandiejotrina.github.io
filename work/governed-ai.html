Case study · Responsible AI

Putting generative AI to work in a regulated function

In a regulated function, the question is not whether AI can draft the answer. It is who is accountable when it does.

SettingMedical Affairs, global medical device company
My roleDesigned the solutions and the governance, led delivery
ToolsMicrosoft Copilot Studio, AI Builder, Power Automate, Dataverse
ResultA reusable pattern for AI with human review

Examine The complaint

A shared mailbox received a steady stream of proctor and physician email. Clinical recommendations were buried in free text, turnaround was hard to see, and checking those recommendations against procedure outcomes was slow manual work.

Diagnose What AI should and should not own

Reading, sorting, matching, and summarizing unstructured text is work AI does well. Sending an answer on behalf of a medical device company is not something it should do alone. The design had to put AI on the first and keep people firmly on the second.

Prescribe and Treat Three solutions, one pattern

  • Compliance checker. Generative AI extracts clinical recommendations from proctor emails, matches them to procedure outcome data in the CRM, and classifies compliance. Built through proof of concept on de-identified test data, with an engineered prompt library and full documentation.
  • Mailbox intelligence. Matches each incoming email to its original request and scores sentiment, giving leadership a request-to-resolution view of turnaround time and physician experience.
  • Copilot agent with a review gate. Drafts replies from an approved knowledge source. A person reviews every reply before it is sent. Nothing goes out on the agent's authority.

Follow Up What carried forward

The pattern is the asset: approved knowledge source, de-identified data for testing, documented prompts, and a human review gate before anything leaves the building. It gives a regulated team a way to say yes to AI without giving up accountability.

The lesson: governance is not the brake on AI adoption in regulated work. It is what makes adoption possible.
