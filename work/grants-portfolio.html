Case study · Portfolio modernization

Buy versus build for an eight-figure grants portfolio

A medical education grants and investigator-initiated studies program was running on manual, hard-to-audit work. The obvious answer was a custom build. It was the wrong one.

SettingMedical Affairs, global medical device company
My roleOwned it end to end, from business case to migration
ScopeGrants, investigator-initiated studies, physician records
Result2,300+ manual hours retired a year

Examine The complaint

Requests, reviews, and approvals moved through manual steps that were slow and difficult to defend in an audit. The team was absorbing more than 2,300 hours a year of work that a system should do.

Diagnose The real decision

The question was not whether to modernize. It was how. A custom build on the existing CRM looked familiar and flexible. A purpose-built platform looked less flexible but was proven for this exact work. I built the business case on total cost, time to value, change burden, and compliance fit, not on preference.

Prescribe The call

  • Buy, not build. The purpose-built option came in at about one third lower three-year cost.
  • Implementation in roughly 4 months instead of an estimated 13 for the custom build.
  • Contract terms tied to service levels, with a clear exit if the vendor did not deliver.

Treat The rollout

I led vendor selection, contract negotiation, implementation, and the migration of historical data out of the CRM, so the team started on the new system with its full history intact.

Follow Up The result

More than 2,300 hours a year of manual, non-compliant work retired, and a portfolio that can now be reported on and audited from one place.

The lesson: the most expensive build is the one you did not need. Score the options on cost, change, and compliance before anyone writes a line of code.
