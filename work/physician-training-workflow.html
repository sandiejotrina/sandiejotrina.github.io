Rebuilding a physician training workflow end to end

Physician training is how a medical device reaches patients safely. The process around it ran on inboxes, spreadsheets, and memory.

SettingMedical Affairs, global medical device company
My roleDesigned the workflow and led delivery
ConstraintsFDA 21 CFR Part 11, Sunshine Act, audit trail
ResultPart of a 70% cut in manual processes

Examine The complaint

Every physician training event needed an expert proctor confirmed, scheduled, reminded, followed up, and paid. Each step was a manual handoff. The symptom people noticed was slow turnaround. The real risk was an incomplete record in a function that has to prove what happened.

Diagnose Where it broke

Mapping the actual flow showed the breaks sat at the handoffs, not with the people. Confirmations lived in email. Case reviews and response times were tracked in manual logs. Finance had no clean, auditable basis for proctor payments. More training would not have fixed any of it. This was a system gap.

Prescribe What to automate, what to keep

  • Automate the predictable, repetitive steps: confirmations, calendar invitations, reminders, surveys, and follow up.
  • Digitize training attestations so the record is created as the work happens, not reconstructed later.
  • Keep judgment with people: clinical decisions and exceptions stay with the team.

Treat What we built

One workflow on the Microsoft stack (Power Automate, SharePoint, Microsoft Forms, DocuSign), built by my developer to my design and validated against audit trail and quality review requirements. Case review tracking and physician response time are captured automatically, which gave Finance an accurate, auditable basis for payments.

Follow Up How we know it worked

Power BI dashboards put proctoring, physician training, and event data in front of executives. The workflow is one of the main contributors to a 70% reduction in manual processes across Medical Affairs operations.

The lesson: the slow part of a regulated process is rarely the people. It is the handoffs nobody owns. Fix the handoff and the audit trail comes for free.
