Case study · Adoption and enablement

Making new tools stick with the people who use them

A rollout is not finished when the system goes live. It is finished when people use it without thinking about it. Two programs, two companies, one rule.

SettingsEnvista (global dental technology), Sonova (hearing technology)
My roleLed rollouts and enablement; built training programs
AudiencesDiverse, often non-technical teams across regions
Results45% adoption lift; 63% new hire success

Global rollouts Envista

Global SaaS platforms were being deployed across regions into teams with very different levels of technical comfort. Deployment alone was not producing use.

  • Simplified the IT complexity teams had to deal with before they could get value.
  • Paired every deployment with enablement designed for the people receiving it, not for the people who built it.
  • Ran technical escalations with hospital IT and product engineering so go-live dates held.

Result: adoption improved 45% across the rollouts.

Onboarding Sonova

New hires had to learn a hearing technology product line that combines hardware and software, and become credible with clinicians quickly.

  • Built scalable onboarding and product training programs for new hires and field teams.
  • Ran Voice of Customer programs that fed field observations back into product design and adoption strategy.
  • Used learning data to keep improving the programs instead of treating launch as the finish line.

Result: new hire success rates improved 63%.

Follow Up Why this matters for AI

AI programs fail the same way platform rollouts fail: the tool ships, the habits do not change, and no one measures it. Follow Up is the step most teams skip, and it is where transformation is won or lost.

The lesson: design the enablement for the people receiving the change, then measure use, not launches.
